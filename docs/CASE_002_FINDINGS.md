# Case 002: Southern Trust's July 2014 algorithm invoice document

**Status:** Preliminary document inventory, November 6, 2026.  
**Question:** What can an invoice document establish about estate-planning-related algorithms and payments?

## Primary source

[EFTA00585292](https://tommycarstensen.com/epstein/documents/EFTA00585292.html), a one-page document dated July 15, 2014, formatted as an invoice issued by Southern Trust Company, addressed to Leon Black. It describes a fee of **$20,000,000** in connection with algorithms for estate planning and related purposes. A recipient line contains a bracketed, unfilled instruction to insert the Black entity to be invoiced.

## What we can say

- The document exists and describes a claimed category of services.
- It names the recipient and a dollar amount.
- It contains an unfilled recipient-entity placeholder, making a finalized contract/invoice status uncertain.
- The record, on its own, **does not prove payment, code delivery, model operation, ownership, criminal conduct, or that the recipient accepted the claim.**

## What would resolve the question?

1. A final executed engagement letter naming the precise company and service.
2. Payment records or settlement confirmation rather than a billing request.
3. Deliverable descriptions, technical specifications, software inventories, and acceptance records.
4. Evidence from both parties' contemporary records, including contrary evidence.

The February 2015 Novamente **$30,000** genetics software invoice in Case 001 describes a different service and counterparty. It must **not** be added to the 2014 $20 million document as though both represent paid purchases or a single project.

Pattern Atlas records the source and the limitation, not an assertion of wrongdoing.

Do not reproduce any banking identifiers from source images.

## MK6 companion record: EFTA01188213

A source in the February 2013 Southern Trust / Leon Black estate-planning correspondence is archived as [EFTA01188213](https://efta.threats.watch/documents/EFTA01188213). It describes a **revised draft**, not an established executed agreement, with conditional fees totaling **$23.5 million**. The archive indexes it on February 11, 2013; its header identifies February 6 revisions to a February 5 draft.

This is **not** the same document as the July 15, 2014 **$20 million** algorithms invoice. Shared names and broad subject matter warrant comparison, but **not summing the dollar figures or asserting that one contract became the other**. The relevant original agreement, amendments, payment records and deliverables remain open questions.

This is stronger receipt-related evidence than the July 2014 invoice alone. The same date and nominal amount are notable. Yet the email excerpt does not specify a payment allocation to the particular algorithm invoice, the invoice's recipient-entity field is unfilled, and neither document demonstrates algorithm delivery or operation.
